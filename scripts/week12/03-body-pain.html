High-Yield Content

Two patients who "hurt all over": fibromyalgia has normal inflammatory markers and pain everywhere; PMR is an older patient with shoulder and hip girdle stiffness, high ESR/CRP, and a dramatic response to prednisone.

Fibromyalgia: what it is

  • Widespread pain (multiple body areas) for more than 3 months, with other somatic symptoms.
  • Mechanism: central sensitization — the pain is "centralized". Any mix of peripheral (nociceptive), neuropathic and central pain can coexist in one person.

Clues that pain is centralizing:

  • Diffuse or multifocal, waxes and wanes, often migratory
  • Sounds "neuropathic"; touch or tight clothing hurts
  • Pain in other body regions earlier in life
  • Comorbid CNS symptoms: fatigue, poor sleep, memory problems, mood
  • Global sensory hyper-responsiveness: light, noise, odours; a "pan-positive" review of systems

Fibromyalgia: diagnosis

  • ACR 1990 criteria — be aware, then forget: ≥3 months of pain above and below the waist, both sides and axial, plus 11 of 18 tender points (4 kg pressure).
  • ACR 2010/2016 criteria — know these. Tender points are replaced by a widespread pain index (WPI, 0–19 areas) and a symptom severity score (SS, 0–12: fatigue, unrefreshing sleep, cognitive symptoms, other somatic symptoms). FM if all of:
    1. WPI ≥7 and SS ≥5, or WPI 4–6 and SS ≥9
    2. Generalized pain: at least 4 of 5 regions (jaw, chest and abdomen don't count)
    3. Symptoms at a similar level for at least 3 months
    4. The diagnosis is valid regardless of other diagnoses
  • WPI + SS together = the polysymptomatic distress scale; FM typically scores >12.

What the clinic visit sounds like:

  • Central pain: "I hurt everywhere", "it hurts to be hugged", light, noise and smells bother me
  • Symptom severity: fatigue cutting activities, "fog" and forgetting, unrefreshing sleep, a "yo-yo" pattern of overdoing it then crashing
  • Somatic symptoms: headaches, TMJ pain, IBS, irritable bladder, non-cardiac chest pain, morning stiffness with no swollen joints

Comorbidity and triggers: more common with any rheumatologic disease, IBD, psychiatric disease, MS, war or disasters, weeks of acute pain (MVA, trauma) and infections (hepatitis B/C, HIV, Lyme, EBV).

Fibromyalgia is NOT a diagnosis of exclusion. It's common to have FM plus something else. Nociceptive pain can centralize over time. Immunologic drugs for RA, SLE or Sjögren's don't improve the FM features.

What to rule out (targeted, common sense):

  • Muscle pain → CK
  • Neuropathic complaints → metabolic workup
  • Joint complaints → examine for swelling
  • Fatigue → CBC, ferritin, TSH, CRP, ALT, creatinine, albumin, sleep study

Fibromyalgia: the science

  • Descending inhibitory pathway (NE, 5-HT, opioids) is attenuated. Diffuse noxious inhibitory control is absent or weak. CSF shows less NE and 5-HT metabolites. Any drug that raises NE and 5-HT improves FM pain.
  • Facilitatory pathway (glutamate via NMDA, substance P) is upregulated. Temporal summation is exaggerated; CSF substance P and glutamate are high.

Fibromyalgia: management

  • Family doctors should diagnose FM early, without specialist confirmation.
  • Non-drug first:
    1. Education
    2. Exercise (weight bearing or not, tai chi, yoga, warm pool)
    3. CBT: sleep hygiene, pacing ("don't overdo it"), mindfulness
    4. Treat nociceptive inputs: PT for back pain, joint injections for OA, treat RA, IBD, endometriosis
    5. Treat sleep apnea
    6. Treat psychiatric illness
  • Drugs that target the brain:
Class Examples Notes

| SNRI | Duloxetine (best evidence), venlafaxine | Works with or without depression; not helpful for sleep | | Gabapentinoids | Pregabalin, gabapentin | Helps sleep | | Low-dose TCAs | Amitriptyline, nortriptyline, cyclobenzaprine | Improve pain, fatigue and sleep; no effect on mood at low dose |

  • Tricks: emphasize non-drug therapy; one drug at a time, lowest dose, titrate slowly, see the patient regularly, set expectations, consider combinations.
  • Avoid opioids: non-opioid users did better on sleep, disability and pain interference, with no difference in pain severity.
  • Expectations: about 50% of patients get a 30% improvement in pain. Pregabalin: NNT 9 for 30% relief; NNH 17 to stop for side effects (somnolence, dizziness, weight gain, nausea).
  • Take-home: FM is real, common, chronic, managed by family doctors, and treatment is multimodal and targets the brain.

Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR)

Typical case: a 70-year-old woman with 3 months of worsening morning pain and stiffness in the shoulders, fatigue, poor appetite and 10 lb weight loss. Normal exam. Labs: anemia (Hb 103), thrombocytosis (489), normal WBC, ESR 73, CRP 65.

Features:

  • Age >50; more common in women and people of Northern European descent
  • Insidious pain and stiffness of shoulder and hip girdles, with profound morning stiffness and fatigue
  • Systemic: weight loss, high ESR/CRP, anemia, thrombocytosis
  • Nothing wrong with the muscles: no weakness, normal muscle histology. Imaging shows inflammation of the joints, bursae and tendons around the shoulder and hip. Cause unknown.

How PMR differs from look-alikes:

Condition Distinguishing feature

| Fibromyalgia | Normal inflammatory markers; stiffness everywhere, proximal and distal | | Myositis | Proximal weakness, usually no pain | | Inflammatory arthritis | Distal joints; reduced passive and active ROM | | OA | Minimal stiffness; worse with activity | | Cancer, infection | Asymmetric; warmth, swelling, other symptoms |

None of these improve as fast with steroids as PMR: expect about 90% better in 48 hours (the case patient was nearly normal in 12 hours).

Treatment:

  • Prednisone 15–20 mg/day, held until the patient is well and the markers normalize, then a slow wean.
  • Dr. Anwar's wean: 15 mg × 1 month → 12.5 mg × 1 month → 10 mg × 1 month → then 1 mg per month; total about 16–18 months. (Dr. Boyd's treatment module gives 15–20 mg, a quicker taper to 10 mg, then 1 mg every 4 weeks — same idea.)
  • Average steroid course is about 2 years; some need low dose longer; relapses are common.
  • Always reassess for other causes of pain. Never treat a high ESR/CRP alone.
  • PMR with GCA symptoms needs high-dose steroids (see the treatment module).

Osteoprotection: anyone expected to take >7.5 mg prednisone for >3 months: calcium 1,500 mg/day, vitamin D 1,000–2,000 IU/day, and an anti-resorptive drug.
