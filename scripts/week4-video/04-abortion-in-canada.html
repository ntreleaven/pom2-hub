Abortion in Canada — Video Script

Week 4 | Reproduction Block | Source: Dr. Stephanie Black, "Abortion in Canada" (Abortion in Canada 2024.pdf), Elentra Learning Objectives

Duration target: ~11-13 min narration

Learning Objectives

  • Review pregnancy termination options in Canada
  • Compare options based on gestational age and describe risks of abortion
  • Compare risks of termination vs. risk of pregnancy
  • Outline counseling regarding fertility and contraception

Definitions first

Abortion means termination of pregnancy by removal or expulsion of a fetus or embryo from the uterus. Spontaneous abortion is what patients call a miscarriage; therapeutic abortion is an induced termination. Obstetrically, a loss under 20 weeks or 500 grams is an abortion; beyond that threshold it's classified as a stillbirth — though under the Vital Statistics Act, any sign of life at birth is a live birth regardless of gestational age or weight.

Scale and context

Roughly 97,000 abortions were reported in Canada in 2022. Globally, about 11.6 per 1,000 women undergo termination, and nearly half of those are classified as unsafe — 97% of unsafe abortions occur in developing countries. In North America, close to half of pregnancies are unintended, and about half of those are terminated. It's estimated that by age 45, 35% of American women will have had an abortion at some point — worth knowing because it reframes this as a common part of reproductive life, not a rare event.

A brief legal history in Canada

1969: abortion permitted only in hospital, only with a committee of physicians agreeing the pregnancy endangered the mother's life or health. 1988: the Supreme Court struck down the law entirely in R v Morgentaler, finding it violated Section 7 Charter rights to life, liberty, and security of the person. 1989: the Court ruled a father has no legal veto over a woman's abortion decision. Mifepristone was approved by Health Canada in 2015 and became available in 2017, with the physician-registration restriction later lifted entirely. Today, Canada is one of very few countries with no federal law restricting abortion — it's regulated like any other medical procedure, governed provincially.

Access still varies enormously by geography — gestational age limits range from 12+0 weeks in Nunavut up to 24+6 in BC, with Ontario at 23+6. It's a big country, and equitable access remains a real, live issue.

Ontario practicalities

OHIP covers abortion at both hospitals and clinics. Surgical abortion is available up to 24+6 weeks; induction abortion beyond 25 weeks is very limited. No referral is required. Pre-procedure evaluation includes confirming an intrauterine pregnancy and gestational age by ultrasound, a medical/surgical history, Rh status, informed consent, and contraceptive counselling.

Methods by gestational age

First trimester (88% of cases):

  • Medical: mifepristone plus misoprostol (brand name Mifegymiso) — or, as an alternative, methotrexate plus misoprostol.
  • Surgical: manual vacuum aspiration (MVA) or D\&C.

Second trimester (about 2% of cases):

  • Surgical: dilation and evacuation (D\&E), generally performed up to 25 weeks.
  • Medical: misoprostol induction, sometimes with a feticidal injection beforehand.

Medical vs. surgical — the tradeoffs

Medical abortion avoids an invasive procedure and anesthesia, is patient-controlled and private, and is available earlier in gestation — but it takes days to weeks to complete, requires committed follow-up, and bleeding is often perceived as heavier. Surgical abortion is a predictable, fast, provider-performed procedure with a high success rate and no required follow-up, but does need an invasive procedure and possibly sedation.

Mifepristone-misoprostol in detail

Mifepristone is an anti-progestin — it binds the progesterone receptor without activating it, causing decidual necrosis, cervical softening, and increased uterine contractility, with effects peaking 36–48 hours later. Misoprostol, a prostaglandin E1 analogue, follows and drives cervical ripening plus increased uterine tone through a cascade involving reduced cAMP and increased collagen breakdown. Candidates for medical abortion need to be committed to follow-up, able to tolerate potential delay, emotionally stable, and willing to proceed to a surgical procedure if the medical method fails. Typical regimen: mifepristone 200mcg, then misoprostol 800mcg 24–48 hours later. 90% will abort within 24 hours of the misoprostol dose. Fewer than 5% ultimately require surgical evacuation for a failed medical abortion.

The methotrexate-misoprostol alternative works differently — methotrexate blocks dihydrofolate reductase, halting DNA synthesis primarily in the cytotrophoblast (not the embryo directly), which stops implantation. It's used up to 49 days gestation, with a structured follow-up of serial beta-hCG measurements to confirm success (a 50% drop by day 3, or an 80% drop by 7–14 days, indicates success; a plateau means the procedure failed and surgical intervention is needed).

Surgical methods in detail

Manual vacuum aspiration is an office-based procedure with a rigid or flexible cannula, done under paracervical block, appropriate up to 10 weeks gestation, with a very low failure rate (0.25%). D\&C is essentially the same concept under 14 weeks, using an electric suction machine, with cervical preparation (misoprostol or osmotic dilators like laminaria) reducing the need for mechanical dilation — which in turn reduces discomfort and infection risk. Key complications to know: cervical shock (a transient vasovagal reaction to the paracervical block), uterine perforation, hemorrhage, and hematometra (post-abortal syndrome — blood accumulating in the uterus, requiring prompt re-evacuation).

Second-trimester D\&E is recognized as the method with the fewest complications compared to induction, with a shorter procedure time and fewer retained products — but the biggest barrier in Canada isn't clinical, it's workforce: there simply aren't enough practitioners trained to perform it safely.

Risk comparison: abortion vs. continuing pregnancy

Across the literature, first-trimester abortion — whether medical or surgical — carries a lower complication rate than continuing a pregnancy to term. This is the single most important risk-communication point for counselling: termination, performed safely, is not the higher-risk option.

Contraceptive counselling after abortion

Ovulation can resume as early as two weeks after a first-trimester abortion, so contraception needs to start immediately, not "at the next period." All methods should be reviewed with the patient — and if a prior method failed (unplanned pregnancy on the pill, for example), that's a strong cue to discuss switching rather than repeating the same method. Common reasons methods fail in practice: cost, difficulty remembering pills, needle phobia limiting DMPA uptake, partner non-compliance with condoms, or a medical contraindication to estrogen. Same-day IUD insertion is effective and convenient, though it carries a slightly higher expulsion rate (about 3.8%) than interval insertion. Same-day DMPA is also effective, and same-day tubal ligation can be offered when general anesthesia is already being used and the patient has completed childbearing.

Fertility and emotional outcomes after abortion

The most common emotional reactions are relief, transient guilt, sadness, and a sense of loss — a mixed and usually manageable picture, not uniform trauma. Performed safely, abortion carries no greater risk to future fertility than a D\&C for miscarriage. The one specific risk worth knowing is Asherman's syndrome (intrauterine adhesions, sometimes causing secondary amenorrhea) — risk is around 8% with a single curettage and rises to about 30% with a third consecutive procedure within 24 hours. Trauma from instrumentation, not infection, is thought to be the primary driver.

Common myths, addressed directly

Abortion does not cause breast cancer. Abortion does not cause infertility when performed safely. It is not illegal in Canada — there is no federal law restricting it at all. Not all patients seeking abortion are single, and patients from religious backgrounds do seek and obtain abortions. And products of conception are not used in makeup or unethical research — that claim has no basis.

Closing

Abortion in Canada is unrestricted at the federal level and treated as routine medical care, though access still varies significantly by province and gestational age. First-trimester options — medical or surgical — are both safe and highly effective, and the choice generally comes down to patient preference and timing rather than one being categorically superior. The framing to carry into practice: termination performed safely carries less risk than continuing an unwanted pregnancy, contraception needs to start immediately afterward, and most patients' emotional experience — relief mixed with some sadness — is normal, not a sign that something went wrong.
