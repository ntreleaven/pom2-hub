High-Yield Content

Why this matters: connective tissue is everywhere, so these diseases hit many organs. They share autoantibodies, Raynaud's and a strong female predominance, and the clinical picture tells you which one you're looking at.

The family

Disease Prevalence
SLE 1 in 1,000
Primary Sjögren's About 2 in 10,000
Systemic sclerosis 1 in 10,000
Polymyositis / dermatomyositis 1 in 100,000

All are more common in women, often with autoantibodies, Raynaud's and multi-organ disease. SLE is more common than scleroderma.

SLE: the classic case

A 22-year-old student with a malar rash, rash in the sun, fatigue, Raynaud's, weight loss, hair loss and morning hand stiffness with swollen knuckles. Labs: WBC 2.8, Hgb 111, platelets 43, normal creatinine and urine, ANA 1/320 speckled, anti-Sm positive, anti-DNA negative, low C3 and C4.

Initial labs: CBC with differential, ESR, creatinine, urinalysis, ANA as the screen, maybe ENA panel, complement. Don't order anti-DNA unless ANA is positive.

The 11 ACR criteria

Classification criteria were made for research. To classify as SLE: at least 4 of 11. The 2019 EULAR/ACR criteria need ANA of at least 1/80 and a score of at least 10.

  1. Malar rash: fixed erythema over the cheeks, photosensitive, spares the nasolabial folds (rosacea doesn't)
  2. Discoid rash: raised patches with adherent keratotic scale and follicular plugging, heals with scarring
  3. Photosensitivity
  4. Oral or nasal ulcers: often multiple, sometimes painless
  5. Inflammatory arthritis: non-erosive
  6. Serositis: pleuritis (worse on inspiration) or pericarditis (worse lying down)
  7. Renal: glomerulonephritis, with protein and blood in urine
  8. Neurologic: seizures or psychosis, without drug or metabolic cause
  9. Hematologic: hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, thrombocytopenia, usually from autoantibodies
  10. Positive ANA
  11. Immunologic: anti-DNA, anti-Sm or antiphospholipid antibodies

Lupus nephritis classes: 1 minimal change; 2 mesangial; 3 focal proliferative; 4 diffuse proliferative (worst); 5 membranous.

Raynaud's and alopecia are common in SLE but not ACR criteria.

ANA: over 95% of SLE is ANA positive. A negative ANA makes SLE highly unlikely; a positive one never rules it in. The most common cause of a positive ANA is no obvious cause. Anti-dsDNA can't occur with a negative ANA.

Other skin: subacute cutaneous lupus (nonscarring, photosensitive, usually anti-Ro positive, face often spared), vasculitic rashes, livedo reticularis.

SLE epidemiology and mechanism

  • 9:1 women to men, young people, 1 in 1,000
  • Drug-induced lupus is rare and milder (joints, skin, serositis)
  • Genes: HLA-DR2 and DR3, complement deficiencies, polygenic
  • Environment: smoking, infection, UV light, silica, hormones, some drugs
  • Immune: B cell hyperreactivity and autoantibodies, T cell abnormalities, immune complexes, type I interferon
  • "More garbage, garbage men on partial strike": increased apoptosis and delayed clearance of nuclear debris

SLE treatment

Depends on the organ involved and severity (minor is skin, joints, mucosa).

Non-drug: education, rest, sun avoidance and SPF 30+, PT and OT, rheumatology follow-up, and cardiac risk reduction (smoking, BP, lipids, diabetes).

Drugs:

  • Hydroxychloroquine for every patient unless contraindicated: the anchor drug, helps skin and joints, may protect against major flares
  • NSAIDs: joint symptoms
  • Corticosteroids: topical for rash, low dose for mild to moderate arthritis, high dose for severe organ disease
  • Immunosuppressants: mycophenolate or azathioprine; methotrexate for skin and joints; cyclophosphamide only for serious major organ disease
  • Biologics: belimumab (decreases B cell signalling)

Prognosis: worse with major organ disease. Deaths from infection, then accelerated atherosclerosis (50-fold more cardiovascular events), SLE itself and drug complications. Steroids cause AVN and osteoporosis.

Antiphospholipid syndrome

Antibodies attack phospholipids on cells and vessel linings, causing clots. Defined by recurrent arterial or venous thrombosis or recurrent pregnancy loss with persistent antibodies.

Antibodies: anticardiolipin; lupus anticoagulant (false PTT prolongation); false-positive VDRL.

Half of APS is associated with SLE (secondary); 10–20% of SLE patients have APS. Often livedo reticularis and low platelets.

Treatment:

  • Antibody positive, no events: nothing, or ASA 81 mg
  • Thrombosis: long-term warfarin
  • Recurrent fetal loss: ASA plus heparin
  • Catastrophic APS ("thrombotic storm"): IV heparin and IV steroids; consider plasmapheresis or IVIG

Sjögren's syndrome

Lymphocytic infiltration of exocrine glands, with dry eyes and mouth (sicca).

  • Eyes: dry, gritty, painful, worse through the day
  • Mouth: trouble with dry foods, altered taste, halitosis, dental caries, parotid enlargement
  • Extra-glandular: Raynaud's, leukocytoclastic vasculitis, arthritis, neuropathy, rarely ILD
  • Higher risk of B cell MALT lymphoma
  • Labs: anemia, leukopenia, polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia, high ESR, ANA 80–90%, RF 90%, anti-Ro and anti-La

Inflammatory myositis

Proximal muscle weakness of shoulders and hips.

Causes of proximal weakness:

  • Drugs: steroids, colchicine, hydroxychloroquine, cyclosporine, statins
  • Toxins: alcohol
  • Endocrine: thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal
  • Electrolytes: potassium, low phosphate, low magnesium
  • Infiltrative (sarcoid), infectious, inflammatory, malignancy-related
Dermatomyositis Polymyositis
Age Peaks in children and older adults Any
Skin Rash, photosensitivity None
Cancer May be paraneoplastic in the elderly Usually not

Dermatomyositis signs: heliotrope, Gottron's papules (raised over knuckles) and sign (flat), V-neck and cape rash, mechanic's hands, dilated nailfold capillaries, calcinosis.

Labs: elevated CK, ESR raised in half, ANA 80%. Anti-Jo-1 correlates with ILD, carries a bad prognosis and is very specific.

Antisynthetase syndrome: myositis, arthralgia, Raynaud's, mechanic's hands, ILD.

Concerning features: esophageal (aspiration), cardiac (arrhythmia), respiratory (ILD, diaphragm), malignancy (ovarian, gastric, lung, cervical).

Treatment: high-dose steroids, then steroid-sparing drugs (azathioprine, methotrexate, cyclophosphamide, IVIG, rituximab). Treat lung disease aggressively.

Systemic sclerosis

Fibrosis, vascular instability, autoimmunity. Has the highest mortality of the CTDs.

Limited (lcSSc) Diffuse (dcSSc)
Skin Distal to elbows and knees, may include fingers and face Also upper arms, thighs, trunk
ANA Over 95% Over 95%; 50% nucleolar
Antibody Anti-centromere (50%) Scl-70 (30%): pulmonary fibrosis. RNA polymerase: renal crisis
Organs Rare renal and heart Higher mortality
Lung PAH with long-standing disease PAH possible

CREST: Calcinosis, Raynaud's, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasia.

Causes of death: pulmonary fibrosis, pulmonary hypertension, cardiac disease.

Scleroderma renal crisis: high BP, intravascular hemolysis, worsening renal function. Mortality over 80% if missed. Highest risk in early progressive diffuse disease. Treat with rapid BP control using ACE inhibitors.

Lung: ILD at the bases; PAH with dilated pulmonary arteries.

Raynaud's phenomenon: reversible pallor, then rubor or cyanosis. 3% of the population, usually primary. Over 95% of scleroderma. Raynaud's plus dilated nailfold capillaries plus anti-centromere: 1 in 3 develop SSc within 5 years.

Raynaud's treatment: cold avoidance, smoking cessation, calcium channel blockers (nifedipine), then PDE5 inhibitors, topical nitrates, IV prostacyclins.

SSc treatment: treat the organs involved; maybe methotrexate in early diffuse disease; stem cell transplant if very severe.

Quick match

  • Proximal weakness, high CK: polymyositis. Add knuckle rash: dermatomyositis
  • Dry eyes and mouth, caries, parotid swelling: Sjögren's
  • Rash in sun, oral ulcers: SLE
  • Dysphagia, Raynaud's, fingertip ulcers, sclerodactyly: systemic sclerosis
  • Repeated clots, livedo: APS
  • ILD, Raynaud's and positive ANA aren't specific

⚠ DMARD toxicity (not on these slides)

Standard teaching to check against the course: hydroxychloroquine (retinal toxicity, so eye screening); methotrexate (liver toxicity, cytopenias, teratogenicity); azathioprine (cytopenias); cyclophosphamide (marrow suppression, bladder toxicity). Biologics raise infection risk.
