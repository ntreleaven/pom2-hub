High-Yield Content

The one rule to carry out of this module: the face has two motor nerves and one main sensory nerve. CN VII moves the face; CN V3 chews; CN V (V1, V2, V3) feels. Almost every facial-nerve question is a version of that.

1. Facial skeleton and foramina

  • The viscerocranium (facial skeleton) holds the jaws and nose and helps form the orbit.
  • Main facial bones: nasal, maxilla, zygomatic, mandible. The frontal bone forms the forehead but isn't technically a facial bone.
  • Three foramina carry nerves and vessels onto the face, lined up vertically:
    • Supraorbital foramen/notch → supraorbital nerve (V1)
    • Infraorbital foramen → infraorbital nerve (V2)
    • Mental foramen → mental nerve (V3)

2. Muscles of facial expression (CN VII)

These develop as a sheet over the face, scalp and neck, then split into sphincters and dilators around the eyes, nose and mouth.

Muscle Action

| Frontalis | Raises the eyebrows | | Occipitalis | Moves the scalp | | Orbicularis oculi | Closes the eye | | Orbicularis oris | Closes the mouth | | Zygomaticus major | Pulls the mouth into a smile | | Buccinator | A facial expression muscle, but important in chewing (keeps food between the teeth) | | Platysma | Helps depress the mandible |

3. Muscles of mastication (CN V3)

They act on the temporomandibular joint (TMJ). The two seen on the face:

  • Masseter: strongly closes the jaw and protrudes it.
  • Temporalis: strongly closes the jaw and retracts it.

Slide question: "What nerve innervates the muscles of mastication?" → the mandibular division of the trigeminal (V3), not the facial nerve.

4. Vessels

Arteries mostly come from the external carotid:

  • Facial artery: runs diagonally from near the angle of the mandible to the medial corner of the eye.
  • Superficial temporal artery: the continuation of the external carotid, running up onto the lateral scalp.

Veins (highly variable):

  • Maxillary + superficial temporal veins → retromandibular vein, which splits into anterior and posterior divisions.
  • Posterior division + posterior auricular → external jugular.
  • Anterior division + facial vein → common facial vein → internal jugular.

5. Parotid gland and duct

  • One of three paired salivary glands; makes saliva for digestion.
  • Lies superficial to masseter, in front of the ear, wrapped in investing fascia.
  • Parotid duct runs superficial to masseter, then pierces buccinator to open into the mouth.
  • The facial nerve passes through the parotid — which is why a parotid tumour can cause an LMN facial palsy.

6. Nerves of the face

Motor:

  • Facial nerve (CN VII) → muscles of facial expression. It enters the parotid and divides into 5 terminal branches: Temporal, Zygomatic, Buccal, Mandibular, Cervical ("To Zanzibar By Motor Car").
  • Trigeminal V3 → muscles of mastication.

Sensory — mostly trigeminal:

Division Skin supplied Named branches on the slide

| V1 ophthalmic | Forehead, upper eyelid, most of the nose | Frontal nerve (supraorbital, supratrochlear) | | V2 maxillary | Midface, lower eyelid, upper lip, lateral to the orbit | Infraorbital nerve | | V3 mandibular | Most of the skin over the mandible, in front of the ear, lateral scalp | Mental, auriculotemporal | | Great auricular (C2, C3) | Skin over the angle of the mandible | — the one part of the face not supplied by V |

7. Predicting symptoms (linking to the cranial nerve module)

  • Can't close the eye, forehead flat, mouth droops on one side → LMN facial nerve lesion (e.g. Bell's palsy, parotid tumour). An UMN lesion (stroke) spares the forehead.
  • Weakness of one branch only (e.g. can't smile on one side after parotid surgery) → injury to that branch within or beyond the parotid.
  • Numb forehead and upper lid; absent corneal reflex → V1. Numb cheek and upper lip → V2 (e.g. an infraorbital fracture). Numb chin and lower lip → V3 (mental nerve).
  • Weak jaw closing, jaw deviates toward the weak side → V3 motor.
  • Numbness at the angle of the jaw → great auricular (cervical plexus), not trigeminal.

8. Facial abnormalities

⚠ Not on this year's slide PDF beyond the structures above. Use the anatomy to reason about common ones: facial asymmetry from CN VII palsy, swelling of the parotid (mumps, stones, tumour), trigeminal neuralgia (shooting pain in a V division), and fractures through the foramina causing numbness in that division.
